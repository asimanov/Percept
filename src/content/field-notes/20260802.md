### Unsolicited then, unsolicited now.

##### Field Notes 20260727-20260802 - Retrospective

<br>

The week ran on deliveries I didn't order. A yellow stencil in the wrong parking garage, the last spot in the building. A fish rising out of a cup. An Iced Earth song I haven't heard in a couple decades, sung soapy-handed in a dream bathroom. Vibrations ringing me like a bell from head to toe about a minute after I'd given up and rolled over to sleep. None of it summoned; all of it received.

**Today's Field Notes illustration:**

> **The Pharos.** The Lighthouse of Alexandria, wonder of the ancient harbor, my rendition assembled from the oldest depictions — the Alexandrian coins and engraved reconstructions that stack it in three tiers, square base to octagonal middle to cylindrical crown, flame at the summit. Medieval accounts add a great mirror beside the fire, said to sight ships still below the horizon while the flame called them home by night — one apparatus that watches and signals, receiver and emitter in the same light. The symbol speaks to fixed vigilance over a moving sea: the tower never chases a hull, and the fire is tended whether or not anything arrives. Its lesson is that the watch is maintenance of flame, not pursuit of ships. Pharos the proper name dissolved into every language's word for lighthouse: the station belongs to everyone; the timing of what makes harbor is mine.

The contrast is what makes this week worth reading back. Every deliberate run at the drop stalled out. Afternoons went to flaccid threshold hunting -- the buildup, the competing tracks of imaginal activity, and the weightless silence teasing a cutover that never came. Meditations stayed on the quiet side. Then lucidity surfaced on its own, in the most ridiculous room available, looking right at me.

The beach said the same thing in a different language. I've spent years listening to engineered waves through headphones, and there I was grounded on sand, listening to the actual waves move in and out. Nothing to chase there either. I retreated the senses one by one, and the moment that made the whole trip worth everything arrived the same way the stencil did -- I looked up and it was just there, the boys playing between my feet, out in the distance.

The nights kept delivering too. Dreams have been vivid and strong all week, and the usual screensaver visuals as I drift about swapped themselves out for lattice structures and geometry, calming and just wild all at once. Then the astral bell: vibrations came up the spine about a minute after I'd quit for the night, and instead of waving them off toward sleep I thought, *eh, whatever happens*, and put my attention on them. In about five minutes I was ringing head to toe. At last I fell asleep, there's no doubt I got out but I have zero recollection of it. This is very common. Either way, the most effective instruction I issued all week was those three words.

Even the practice logistics agreed. The night before a session I asked what to do next and went with the first thing that popped in. The first pass is the correct one -- a simple lesson that takes forever to learn and some more time to get into good practice of following. It takes courage and trust, something we all spend a lot of time repairing here in the physical.

So the week's instruction, assembled from its own deliveries: the buildup doesn't summon the message, and the setting doesn't dignify it. The number belongs to everyone. What belongs to me is the timing. 

The ships are still out. The watch continues.
