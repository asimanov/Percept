### The Mustache Clause.

##### Field Notes of Nobody's Ghost - 20261007-S8

<br>

**The initial meeting of Konpeitō mushroom people went well.**

<br>

---

<br>

Every form that the ghost takes is an outline, and every outline wears a mustache. That is a rule of the lore, and it stays. Will the outline and mustache always be white? I'm leaving that one open.

The one time I saw my own double clearly, I had just sat up out of my body, grayscale and transparent, looking down at myself from more than one place at once. No mustache. I didn't wear one then, but I have worn one since last October.

So the lore contradicts the only observation that I have of my own etheric/energy double. I'm keeping it that way anyway nonetheless, with a condition attached: I *believe* that the double carries the mustache now, and I expect that when I see it again, the mustache will be gone.

The condition is what makes the belief work. Belief is the forward reach, the provisional part, the extension toward what hasn't been touched yet. Its whole job is to be replaced by experience. Held with its exit named, it stays ready to act on and ready to drop. If held as if it were already knowledge, it has no exit, and when experience contradicts it, something within us breaks.

The condition also gives the next sighting a marker. If the double shows up without the mustache, it didn't come from the drawings or the mirror. Something other than my habit of reproducing reference rendered it, and the missing detail is how I'll know.

This runs well past the innocent example above. Every day hands me small things that sit wrong against what I hold true, or, more precisely, what I believe. Each one gets looked at briefly, then kept or set down. The psychiatrist John Mack called it ontological shock when the people he interviewed met something their model of reality had no room for.

My wager is that this kind of shock lands hardest on beliefs being held as knowledge, and that a habit practiced on small things all day keeps the large ones from arriving as a complete collapse of our reality.

Materialists call this falsifiability, I call it belief doing its job. We perceive only a sliver of what is "there", and from either side, holding the sliver lightly is the only reliable tool for the rest.

Apocalypse meant, and still does to me, an unveiling before it meant an ending to many around the world today. The condition is what lets an unveiling register as information rather than the end of times. Speaking of the saying, it's literally **end** of ***times***, it's a change, nothing more.

Something of you stays in every disguise. For the ghost, it's the mustache. I'll find out what it is for me soon enough.
