### Get Outside Your Mind and Let It Go.

##### Field Notes from 20261118: A monk's three floors, Tolle's verb, and one question for trance

<br>

It took me a while to get to this podcast, and I'm glad that I finally did. Dandapani sat down with André Duqum on Know Thyself for over two hours, and I might look up more of his work. What I like most is how he decides to craft his message. I know it's tired to say, because *The Power of Now* is literally the same quality of presentation, but distilling the knowledge of consciousness, awareness and focus to such a degree is very helpful. I always look for this in my own writing, and that's why I like material such as Eckhart Tolle's.

**An aside:** Dandapani is a Hindu priest and former monk who spent ten years in a cloistered monastery in Hawaii under his guru, Sivaya Subramuniyaswami. He teaches focus and is the author of *The Power of Unwavering Focus*.

He only needs two ideas to keep at all times. The mind is a vast space with many areas in it, and awareness is a glowing ball of light that travels through that space. *"The mind doesn't move. Awareness moves within the mind."* Wherever the light goes, that area lights up and you become conscious of it. Anger becomes a place you are standing in, and you can leave as easily as you arrived.

I particularly loved the house. Put a frame around that vast space and the mind is a three-story building. The ground floor is the conscious mind, the instinctive one, busy with the five senses and everything around you. The second floor is the subconscious, the intellect and the storehouse of everything you've taken in. The third floor is the superconscious, where intuition lives. The ball of light is on one floor at a time. Intuition has to come down from the third floor through the second to reach you on the first, and most of us have filled the second floor like a library where a truck drops off boxes of books every day and nobody shelves them. His fix is a filter, the same kind you put over a kitchen drain -- my fix is to purge, repurpose, organize.

He doesn't treat the house as if it has legs and can move around. His insistence is that the mind, much like the house, is stationary, and what moves through it is awareness, the ball of light. A clean analogy for separating the ego, the mind, from the Self, the awareness. While Dandapani doesn't concern himself with anything outside the house, I prefer a different instruction: ***get outside your mind and let it go.***

The practice is one task. Keep the ball of light on one object, and when it drifts, bring it back. The practice goes into what you do every day anyway: talking with your kid, brushing your teeth, eating breakfast. Awareness, he says, should be like a well-trained dog that walks at your side until you say otherwise.

It would be easy to say that Tolle only describes presence while Dandapani hands you the verb. Hard disagree. Tolle might wax poetic about presence because his target is people who need to learn it to begin with. *Practicing the Power of Now* is literally a book on the verb portion, the exercises pulled out of the first book.

Honestly, it's hard to distinguish which of the methods I've found to work. Tolle's *allowing* led me to noticing. An angry outburst gets recognized immediately, and awareness goes right to it to sort out the why and the what. That instantly defuses the emotion and reorients the situation, or the mind rather. After doing that habitually, it becomes like a trained dog. So more or less it feels the same.

Where we part ways is the destination. Dandapani wants the Self and treats everything else that opens up inside as a sidetrack. Asked about the nature of mind, he says he doesn't go down that road. By that measure, I'm wasting time exploring consciousness, though he does say the choice is each person's to make. So of course he doesn't have an answer for my related condition during trance.

In Thursday's entry I wrote about the receive window at the end of an exercise from The Gateway Experience, The Monroe Institute's audio course. You sit in Focus 12, a state of expanded awareness, open to non-verbal communication (NVC), and lately nothing has been coming in. Traditionally, during trance, you're tasked with keeping focus on something: the breath, a point. So if I'm engaged in NVC, maybe I focus on a single image? I don't know. I'm not sure how communication back works if I'm engaged with something other than just being.

---

*An update since I wrote that. On 20261121, in the same receive window, I held an imaginary ball of light a few feet in front of my brow, and it worked. No NVC came my way, but the stillness held. Oldest focus point in the history of meditation and here I am: "oh man, it works!"*
